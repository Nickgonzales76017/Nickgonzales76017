I'm building **Aurekai**, an operating business that develops products and works with existing businesses, creators, publishers and communities. Underneath it is **Bonfÿre**, the native software platform I'm building to handle the parts those businesses have in common.

I've spent a lot of time around existing websites, PHP and SQL systems, business applications and integrations. A company might need to move an old site, keep its reporting intact, connect its customers to a CRM and improve the way staff work. A publisher may need a home for articles, recordings and contributors. A retailer may need product records that work in a catalogue, procurement system and room planner.

Those seem like unrelated projects until you start building them. They need many of the same things: identity, accounts, records, data access, publishing, communications, finance and useful automation.

That's what Aurekai is built around. AI has made it possible to create software much faster, and I want that development work to accumulate into a business that can support more than one product. The company can share the technology while each business or creator keeps its own name, customers and work.

## Aurekai

Aurekai has four public areas. They're places for different kinds of work, not four separate applications.

| Destination | What it's for |
|---|---|
| [Work](https://aurekai.work) | Organizations, jobs, opportunities, business relationships and account applications |
| [Life](https://aurekai.life) | People, profiles, communities, collections and publishing |
| [Live](https://aurekai.live) | Creators, recordings, channels, media and events |
| [Shop](https://aurekai.shop) | Products, suppliers, catalogues, sourcing and commerce |

An independent retailer can also have its own website and customer experience. The [white-label site examples](https://docs.aurekai.work/three-businesses) show what that means for branding, content and access.

The market signals are part of why I'm pursuing this approach. The [U.S. Chamber's 2026 small-business research](https://www.uschamber.com/technology/2026-empowering-small-business-report) found 41% of respondents using at least six technology platforms. [W3Techs' November 2026 survey](https://w3techs.com/technologies/comparison/cm-wordpress) still puts WordPress on 39.4% of measured sites. I want to work with the software and business knowledge people have already built, while developing the new applications they need. I keep a [source-linked research page](https://nickgonzales76017.github.io/bonfyre/market.html) for the other markets Aurekai is entering.

## Bonfÿre

Bonfÿre is the technical foundation behind those products. It brings together Matrix identity and addressing, Source Library, WorkGraph, native commands, Frappe-based application grammars, local models, media tooling, finance and connected web experiences.

The native development estate currently includes 93 command families, 1,840 Frappe-derived DocTypes across 51 modules, and a WorkGraph with 20,487 nodes. Those systems cover much more than the public demos in the GitHub Pages collection.

For a concrete example of how the architecture is intended to work: an external job posting can be retained once, shown in discovery and continued into a CRM or narrated update through the applications an account actually uses. Or a supplier's product can move from a catalogue into a 3D room plan without becoming an unrelated item in each tool.
